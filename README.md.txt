# Communicate Data Findings — U.S. Construction Spending Analysis

## Summary
This project explores 14 years of U.S. construction spending data (2002–2016)
from the U.S. Census Bureau to answer: What does the data reveal about how
private investors allocate capital across sectors, and what do economic shocks
teach us about developer priorities?

## Key Insights for Part II Presentation

1. Total private construction spending fell nearly 45% from peak (909B in 2006)
to trough (500.8B in 2011), with recovery still incomplete by 2016.

2. Not all sectors follow the same cycle — residential crashed immediately in
2008 while office spending actually peaked during the recession due to project
pipeline momentum, crashing with a 2-3 year lag.

3. Manufacturing overtook Commercial as the largest investment sector by the
Growth era (2013-2016), representing a complete reversal of pre-recession
sector hierarchy.

4. Public spending acted as a countercyclical stabilizer — rising from 237.9B
to 311.1B as private investment retreated during the recession.
